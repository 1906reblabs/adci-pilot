## What changed and why

1. **Broadband affordability (INF-02) is back, after three runs withdrawn.** New values come from the ITU 2025 price baskets (data-only, % of GNI per capita): South Africa 1.27, Nigeria 1.56, Kenya 4.1 (ZAF-INF-02-20, NGA-INF-02-20, KEN-INF-02-20). They were read from a mirror of the ITU file, so confidence is medium until checked against the file itself. Kenya is the only pilot country above the 2% target; South Africa, not Nigeria, is cheapest.
2. **Internet use (INF-01) is now one construct for all three, and Nigeria's figure fell from 57.4 to 41.2.** The registry asks for the share of people using the internet. South Africa's old 75.6 was households with mobile access, and Nigeria's was NCC subscriptions per 100 people, which multi-SIM use inflates. Both now use ITU 2024 individual use: ZAF 78.4, NGA 41.2, KEN 35.0 (ZAF-INF-01-20, NGA-INF-01-20, KEN-INF-01-20). Kenya's matches its own Tier-1 survey.

## What else the checks found
- **The ranking is not robust.** Without FIN-02 the scores are ZAF 49.4, NGA 48.5, KEN 48.1. If FIN-01 used Findex 2025 digital-payment shares (ZAF 67, NGA 54, KEN 89), Kenya would rank above Nigeria. FIN-01's definition is now an open decision, not just a recurring note (Findings 3 and 4).
- **AI-01 was misattributed.** The scores (52.15 / 49.7 / 37.7) come from the Qhala and Qubit Hub AI Talent Readiness Index (April 2025), not PwC/Strategy&; confidence is now low (AI-01 sources, Finding 5).
- **Kenya's digital-ID figure may not measure Maisha Namba.** A 2025 progress report cites 90,038 Maisha Numbers issued in four counties against the 13M used since May 2024 (KEN-GOV-01-21). Still no coverage statistic, sixth week.
- **South Africa's ID migration is understated.** Home Affairs says about 14.7M people hold only a green ID book (down from 16M) and the book is invalid from 31 Mar 2028 (ZAF-GOV-01-20). A 34M-cards figure failed validation.
- **Funding context, not scored:** H1 2026 was Nigeria $254M, Kenya $126M, South Africa $83M. Jan–Aug on a different tracker was $528.6M, $216.6M and $248.2M (NGA-ECO-02-21).
