# ADCI Intelligence Brief — Week of 2026-08-26

**Read this before looking at the trend lines.** Kenya's and Nigeria's headline ADCI
scores both dropped materially this week (Kenya 55.7 → 48.8, Nigeria 58.5 → 53.2). Neither
drop reflects a real-world decline. Both are corrections of the two BLOCKING data-quality
findings the Red Team raised last week. Details below.

## Why Kenya's and Nigeria's scores fell

1. **Kenya's Finance pillar (98.0 → 52.6).** Last week's figure measured mobile-money
   *registration* — the Red Team flagged this as blocking. This week's figure is the CBK/
   KNBS/FSD Kenya 2024 FinAccess survey's demand-side finding: 52.6% of Kenyans use mobile
   money *daily*. This is a genuinely different, more honest number — not a smaller version
   of the same overstatement. On a like-for-like usage basis, it's still the *strongest*
   Finance figure of the three pilot countries this week.
2. **Nigeria's Finance pillar (98.7 → 96.7, but the real correction is bigger than that
   looks).** Last week's figure was built on a Chainalysis global crypto-adoption rank of
   #2. That was wrong: Chainalysis's own current report (published September 2025, still
   the current edition) puts Nigeria at #6 of 151. Last week's Source Scout appears to have
   pulled from a stale or mismatched reference. The *converted index values* land close
   together (98.7 vs. 96.7) mainly because the ordinal-to-index formula compresses gaps
   near the top of a long ranked list — the rank correction itself (#2 to #6) is more
   significant than the two-point score change suggests.

Full reasoning is in this week's `red_team.md` (Findings 1 and 2).

## What's new and working well

- **South Africa's Finance pillar has a value for the first time** (7.26, IMF Financial
  Access Survey active mobile-money accounts) — but it's the lowest score in this week's
  entire dataset, and the Red Team flags this as likely a *construct mismatch* rather than
  a real gap: South Africa's digital payments run mainly through bank/card/PayShap rails
  (725 million PayShap transactions, R659bn, since 2023) rather than telco-style mobile
  money, which is specifically what this indicator measures. Read alongside South Africa's
  strong Infrastructure (84.3) and Government (76.2, best of the three) pillars, this looks
  like an indicator-definition gap, not evidence that South Africa lacks digital payments.
- **Government service maturity (GOV-02) is populated for all three countries for the
  first time**, using the UN's 2024 E-Government Survey Online Service Index: South Africa
  88.7, Kenya 77.7, Nigeria 53.7 — the same methodology across all three, so this is a
  genuine, comparable relative gap, not a coverage artifact.
- **Startup ecosystem strength (ECO-01) is populated for all three countries for the first
  time**, using StartupBlink's 2026 Global Startup Ecosystem Index: South Africa 52nd,
  Kenya 61st, Nigeria 62nd (of 120 countries).
- **AI-native company counts (AI-02) are populated for all three countries for the first
  time**: Nigeria leads with 50 tracked AI-native startups, South Africa 49, Kenya 31 — but
  Kenya's AI startups are the most *advanced* by stage-mix (42% in growth phase, vs. 24-25%
  for the other two), a genuine quality-over-quantity signal.
- **Nigeria's actual AI talent-readiness score is now on record** (37.7) — last week only
  had its rank (18th of 54 African countries).

## A big development outside this week's scored indicators

Nigeria led continental *equity* startup funding in H1 2026 ($214M) for the first time
since 2022 — a sharp reversal from the FY2025 story (Nigeria was the only pilot country
with declining VC capital). This wasn't used in this week's ECO-02 figure (which stays on
an annualized FY2025 basis for consistency across countries) but is flagged prominently
for next cycle and in this week's forecast reasoning.

## New methodology this week

Following last week's Red Team request, indicator observations built from country
*rankings* (rather than directly published scores) now use one documented, consistently-
applied formula: `index = 100 × (N − rank) / (N − 1)`, where N is the total number of
countries ranked. This was applied to Nigeria's corrected FIN-02 and to all three
countries' new ECO-01 figures. The Red Team's Finding 6 flags a residual limitation:
this formula is linear in rank, so it doesn't capture whether a one-place rank difference
reflects a large or small real-world gap.

## What still needs fixing

- **South Africa and Kenya are still missing FIN-02** (crypto/stablecoin adoption) —
  Chainalysis's current index doesn't list either in its published top-20 table, and a
  specific rank wasn't located within this cycle's search scope.
- **FIN-01 is measured three different ways across the three countries this week** (Kenya:
  survey usage rate; Nigeria: registered accounts; South Africa: active accounts) — each
  individually well-sourced, but not a clean cross-country comparison this cycle. Red Team
  Finding 3 has the detail.
- **Kenya's digital-ID coverage figure (GOV-01, 40.6%) is low-confidence and dated** (a May
  2024 government statement) — 2026 press coverage keeps citing the same base figure
  without an update, itself flagged this week as a Society-level transparency signal worth
  tracking.
