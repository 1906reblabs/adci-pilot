# Integration Notes — Week of 2026-09-02

## South Africa
This week's evidence sharpens rather than resolves the FIN-01 construct-mismatch story flagged
last cycle. Chainalysis's own Sub-Saharan Africa regional report (checked this cycle) confirms
South Africa as the **#2 crypto market in the region by on-chain dollar volume**, with a
distinctly institutional profile (custody products, large-ticket arbitrage flows) — a picture
of a genuinely mature digital-finance market. That sits directly alongside FIN-01 (7.26), the
lowest score in the entire dataset. Read together, GOV-02 (88.7, best-in-pilot), the Smart ID
bank-distribution story (GOV-01, ongoing), and now this SSA volume ranking all point the same
direction: South Africa's digital-finance and digital-government infrastructure is genuinely
strong, and FIN-01's mobile-money-specific construct is the outlier, not South Africa's
underlying digitization. No new mechanism-level link to trace this cycle beyond reinforcing
last cycle's read.

## Nigeria
A quiet cycle for Nigeria specifically — no indicator changed, and the one open thread from
last cycle (whether the H1 2026 equity-funding reversal, $214M, would show up in a fuller-year
data point) has no new confirmation yet; the only H1 2026 continental figure located this cycle
($1.44B, TC Insights) does not break out by country. Nigeria's FIN-01 registration-based
construct concern (Red Team Finding 5, 2026-08-26) persists unresolved and now directly parallels
this week's Kenya GOV-01 story below: in both cases, a real Tier-1-adjacent data source exists,
but doesn't measure quite what the registry indicator is defined to capture, and no fresher
edition has appeared to fix it.

## Kenya
This week's most notable finding is methodological rather than substantive: last cycle's
recommendation to check immigration.go.ke directly for a primary Maisha Namba coverage figure
(rather than relying on secondary press repeating the same 2024 base) was carried out, and
turned up **no consolidated statistics page at all** on the primary source's own site. This
reframes GOV-01's staleness — it is not that secondary outlets are being lazy about updating a
citation; the primary source itself does not appear to publish a running coverage total. This is
structurally similar to the FIN-01 pattern seen in Nigeria and (before its correction) Kenya
itself: a real government program with real activity (card issuance, backlog clearing) that
currently cannot be captured in a clean, current, population-denominated statistic. Kenya's own
FIN-01 (52.6, usage-based) remains the pilot's strongest, unaffected by any of this — the two
indicators tell genuinely different stories about the same government's digital maturity.
