# Integration Notes — Week of 2026-09-30

Annotation only; no value changed.

## South Africa
The bank-as-identity-issuer link (GOV <-> Finance) is strengthening on the ground: 515 bank branches now offer walk-in smart ID replacement, a hard end date (31 Mar 2028) has been set for the green ID book, and about 14.7M people still hold only a green book (ZAF-GOV-01-20, context). Meanwhile FIN-01 (7.26) cannot see bank, card or PayShap rails, while Findex 2025 shows 67% of adults making digital payments (ZAF-FIN-01-21, not scored). The index still reads the Finance link from the wrong side. Cheap data (INF-02 1.27%) and the highest internet use (78.4%) sit behind the best Government pillar.

## Nigeria
Identity is outpacing use. NIN enrolment is about 142M against a presidential target of 95% coverage by December 2026 (NGA-GOV-01-20), but only about 41% of Nigerians use the internet (NGA-INF-01-20), NCC reports about 27% own smartphones, and Findex has 63% with an account but 54% making digital payments (NGA-FIN-01-22). The new INF-01 reading shows a connectivity ceiling that the old subscription series hid. GOV-02 (53.7, lowest of the three) is the same story from the government side. The earlier hypothesis that an easing naira would moderate FIN-02 while ECO-02 recovered is still untested: FIN-02 stayed at rank 3 while 2026 funding rose ($528.6M Jan-Aug, TechCabal Insights, not annual).

## Kenya
A new link appears between pillars: affordability and use. Kenya is the only pilot country above the 2% data-price target (INF-02 4.1%) and has the lowest internet use (35%), yet 89% of adults made digital payments (KEN-FIN-01-22) and 52.6% use mobile money daily. M-Pesa's rail evidently does not depend on broadband or on the state ID system, which remains unreported (GOV-01, sixth week on a May-2024 base, now with a contradicting 2025 figure). The GOV -> FIN chain still cannot be traced. H1 2026 funding ($126M) points to a cooling digital-economy link; Jan-Aug on another tracker ($216.6M) does not change that read.
