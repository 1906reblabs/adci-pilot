# Integration Notes — Week of 2026-08-26

## South Africa
This week's new GOV-02 (88.7, highest of the three pilot countries) and the continuing
GOV-01 Smart ID story (banks as a distribution channel, per last week's note) now sit
alongside a newly-populated but structurally weak FIN-01 (7.26). Read together, these
three data points tell a coherent story rather than a contradictory one: South Africa's
government and financial-sector *digital infrastructure* is genuinely mature (best-in-pilot
e-government portals, banks actively distributing state ID credentials, SARB's PayShap
processing R659bn since 2023), but that maturity is not visible in FIN-01 because the
indicator is defined around mobile-money-specific usage rather than the bank/card rails
South Africa actually uses. This is the clearest case yet in this pilot of an indicator
construct not fitting a country's actual digital-finance model — see this week's Society
note for South Africa. Watch for whether a future FIN-01 revision (or a supplementary
"digital payment penetration, rail-agnostic" measure) changes South Africa's Finance
pillar materially once GDP-scale bank-rail activity (PayShap, EFT, card) is captured
alongside or instead of mobile-money-specific accounts.

## Nigeria
Three threads converge this week. First, GOV-02 confirms Nigeria has the *lowest*
e-government service maturity of the three pilot countries (OSI 53.7, EGDI rank 144th of
193) — a genuine relative gap, not a data-availability artifact, since GOV-02 used identical
UN methodology across all three countries this cycle. Second, the corrected FIN-02 (rank
#6, down from a data-error-inflated #2) still shows Nigeria near the top of global crypto
adoption, with Chainalysis explicitly attributing this to naira depreciation pushing
citizens toward stablecoins — consistent with last week's Integration note pairing FIN-02
strength with declining dollar-denominated VC (ECO-02). Third, and this may be the most
important development of the week: H1 2026 data shows Nigeria *leading* continental
equity funding for the first time since 2022 ($214M), a sharp reversal of the FY2025
story where Nigeria was the only pilot country with declining VC capital. If naira
conditions are genuinely stabilizing (as this reversal would suggest), watch for FIN-02's
stablecoin-driven strength to *moderate* next cycle even as ECO-02 recovers — a joint
move in the direction the constitution's bias-check anticipates (structural currency
stress easing) rather than the divergent-drivers pattern flagged last week.

## Kenya
This week's evidence resolves last week's most consequential blocking finding (FIN-01)
while simultaneously exposing a related, unresolved gap: Kenya's newly-sourced GOV-01
figure (40.6%) is itself low-confidence and dated, meaning the GOV-01→FIN interoperability
chain this project's README describes (digital ID → payments → fintech) still cannot be
traced with confidence for Kenya, exactly as last week's note observed. What's new this
week is *why* it can't be traced: not just Maisha Namba's legal/institutional friction
(still ongoing per this week's Society note) but an apparent lull in official *reporting*
on coverage progress since mid-2024, even as card issuance and backlog-clearing activity
visibly continues per secondary sources. Meanwhelst, Kenya's corrected FIN-01 (52.6% daily
mobile-money usage) is now the strongest, most credible FIN-01 figure of the three pilot
countries — a reminder that M-Pesa's fintech-native rail continues to outperform on a
like-for-like usage basis regardless of the GOV-01 story. Watch next cycle for whether a
current, population-denominated Maisha Namba figure closes the GOV-01 confidence gap.
